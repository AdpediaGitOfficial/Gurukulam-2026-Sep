Course portfolio and trainer capacity

Rebuilt for 1,000+ courses and 100+ trainers. At that size a ranked list of ten is not a smaller version of the answer — it is a different and mostly wrong one. I loaded a database to that scale and measured what the dashboard does today before designing anything.

Measured first

The dashboard already has this bug, and it is worse than slow

I cloned the database, grew it to 1,217 courses / 291 trainers / 1,267 batches, and called the live endpoint.

Speed was never the problem. The numbers are wrong.

topCourses reads findMany({ take: 50 }) with no ordering, fires four more queries per course, then sorts those and keeps ten. At 1,217 courses that is a ranked list drawn from an arbitrary 4% sample. trainerLoad does the same with 50 of 291.

The proposal

Distribution first, exceptions second, ranking last

Three primitives, in that order. A distribution describes the whole population in fixed space at any size. An exception queue is bounded by what needs action, not by an arbitrary cut-off. A ranked list comes last, with its basis and its denominator stated, because ten of 1,217 is a sample and should say so. Every figure links through to the list view with the filter already applied.

Roughly 25× fewer queries, and the answers are true

Ranking belongs in the database, where ORDER BY … LIMIT sees every row. The exception queues are count() against existing indexes. Nothing here needs a cache, a materialised view or a nightly job — and if the catalogue ever reaches a size where it does, a distribution is the one shape that materialises cleanly.

Three calls, and why

The distribution replaced the per-course bar list. Four bars out of 1,217 courses raises the question "which four, and why those" and answers nothing about the other 1,213. One strip describes every course, in less space, and each segment is a filter.

Course progress uses one hue in four ordered steps; utilisation uses status colour. Delivery is magnitude, so it gets a light-to-dark ramp. Utilisation is different — both ends are the problem, idle and overloaded — so the ends are amber and red and the healthy middle is green. Both ramps went through the palette validator, not my eye.

Every ranked list states its basis and its denominator. "Most loaded — 5 of 291, by sessions ahead" can be checked. "Top trainers" cannot, and is how the current dashboard came to be confidently wrong.

What I'd build, in order

1 — Fix the sampling. topCourses and trainerLoad are wrong right now, at today's size as well as tomorrow's; 50 unordered rows is already a sample of 267 batches. This is a bug fix and it should not wait for the cards.

2 — The two distributions and the exception queues. Seven of the ten figures, no schema change, under 5 ms of database time.

3 — The roster's complete/exit verb, which lights up completion and drop-out. Punctuality and rating wait for the portals, where the people who know those facts will be.
